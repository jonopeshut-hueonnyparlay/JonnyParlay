# picksbyjonny — Paid Tier Research Agenda
# Goal: understand what makes a premium picks service genuinely valuable so the product is top-tier at launch

## Context

picksbyjonny is a sports picks/DFS Discord community run by Jono. The model is analytical and sharp — proprietary projection engine, calibrated probability model, multi-sport (NBA, MLB, WNBA, NHL). Brand positioning: luxury · sharp · analytical. Tagline: *edge > everything*.

The service is preparing to go paid. The question is not "how do I charge money" but "what value do I deliver that makes people trade money for it without hesitation — and keep paying."

Research should be grounded, specific, and cite real examples where possible. Avoid generic advice. Every finding should answer: "so what does this mean for picksbyjonny specifically?"

---

## Section 1 — What subscribers actually pay for

**Questions:**
1. In premium picks communities (Discord, Substack, X), what do surveys and public discussions reveal about WHY people subscribe? Is it the picks themselves, the analysis/reasoning, the community, the brand, or the track record?
2. Do subscribers primarily want to be told what to bet, or do they want to understand why? What does the research say about information vs. instruction as a value driver?
3. What is the #1 reason people cite for upgrading from free to paid in picks services? What triggers the purchase decision?
4. What do subscribers say they're actually getting from paid services that they couldn't get free? What's the perceived scarcity?
5. How much does "exclusive access" (early picks, private channels) drive willingness to pay vs. content quality?

---

## Section 2 — Competitive landscape

**Questions:**
1. Who are the top 5-10 premium sports picks services/cappers operating on Discord right now? What do they charge (monthly, weekly, per-pick, lifetime)? What sports/markets do they cover?
2. What do the best-regarded picks Discords (500+ paying members) offer that low-regarded ones don't? What separates top-tier from average?
3. What price points are common in this market? Is there a price ceiling where churn spikes? Is there a "too cheap looks low-quality" floor?
4. How do the best cappers handle multi-sport coverage — do they go deep on one sport or wide across many? Which positioning resonates more with paying subscribers?
5. What do competitors get wrong that picksbyjonny could do better? What are the most common complaints about paid picks services?
6. Are there picks services that have successfully positioned as "sharp/analytical" (vs. gut feel/tout)? How do they communicate that positioning and does it command a price premium?

---

## Section 3 — Trust and credibility

**Questions:**
1. What are the most effective ways picks services build trust with a skeptical audience? What signals make someone believe a capper is legitimate vs. a tout?
2. How do top services handle losing streaks publicly? What communication strategies retain subscribers vs. cause mass cancellations during bad runs?
3. What role does verified track record play in conversion? Do people check records before subscribing, or does social proof (testimonials, community energy) matter more?
4. How transparent should methodology be? Does explaining the model increase trust, or does it risk appearing like you're over-explaining losses?
5. What is the impact of publicly posting all picks (wins AND losses) vs. cherry-picking? Do subscribers penalize services that hide losses?
6. What do the most trusted cappers do differently in how they present their results and accountability?

---

## Section 4 — Pricing architecture

**Questions:**
1. What pricing structures work best for picks Discord communities: monthly subscription, weekly, per-pick, tiered access, lifetime? What are the retention profiles of each?
2. What is the right free-to-paid content ratio? How much should a free member get to convert them to paid — too little and they don't see the value, too much and they don't upgrade?
3. What price point for a monthly picks subscription feels "premium but accessible" vs "expensive/exclusive" in 2025-26? Is there data on conversion rates at different price points ($25/mo vs $50/mo vs $100/mo)?
4. What add-ons or upsells do top services monetize beyond the base subscription (premium tier, 1-on-1 consults, DFS advice, model access)?
5. How do successful services handle the "I can get free picks everywhere" objection? What makes the paid version worth it when free picks exist?
6. Should pricing be per-sport, all-inclusive, or tiered by sport? What does subscriber behavior research suggest?

---

## Section 5 — Content and presentation beyond the picks

**Questions:**
1. What content do the highest-retention picks services post beyond the picks themselves? Pre-game analysis, market commentary, line movement alerts, post-game recaps?
2. How detailed should pick explanations be? Do subscribers engage more with picks that include reasoning ("proj=28.4 vs line=24.5, strong edge") vs. just the pick ("Mitchell over 24.5 ✅")?
3. What is the role of educational content (how to bet, bankroll management, what CLV means) in retaining subscribers who are newer to sharp betting?
4. How often should a service post to maintain engagement without overwhelming members? What cadence do high-retention Discords use?
5. What visual presentation formats (custom graphics, embed cards, screenshot of slips) drive the most engagement and feel most premium?
6. What is the value of live content — in-game updates, real-time line movement alerts — vs. pre-game only? Do subscribers who get live updates churn less?

---

## Section 6 — Community dynamics and Discord retention

**Questions:**
1. What Discord server structures and channel layouts do the highest-retention picks communities use? What roles/permissions drive engagement?
2. What is the optimal community size for a paid picks Discord? Is there a "too big" where quality degrades, or a sweet spot where community feel is strongest?
3. What community features drive retention beyond the picks (member interaction, community picks channel, leaderboards, accountability threads)?
4. How do top Discords handle bad actors (freerollers, leakers, toxic members) without burning community trust?
5. What is the role of the operator's personal presence (voice chats, regular Q&A, personal responses to members) in retention? Does it scale?
6. What do high-churn picks Discords have in common? What are the most frequent reasons members cancel?

---

## Section 7 — Brand positioning (luxury · sharp · analytical)

**Questions:**
1. What does "luxury" positioning look like in practice for a sports picks service? Who does it well, and what specific design/language/behavior choices make it feel luxury vs. pretentious?
2. How do "sharp" picks services communicate their edge without alienating recreational bettors who are the majority of paying subscribers?
3. What language, tone, and presentation choices reinforce an analytical/data-driven brand vs. feeling cold and unapproachable?
4. What do successful premium brands in adjacent spaces (financial newsletters, DFS tools) do that translates to a sports picks brand?
5. Should picksbyjonny show the model/math publicly or keep it proprietary? What's the positioning tradeoff — transparency builds trust but secrecy creates mystique?
6. How do you communicate "we lose sometimes but we have long-term edge" in a way that sophisticated subscribers believe and recreational subscribers accept?

---

## Section 8 — Subscriber journey (free → paid → retained)

**Questions:**
1. What is the typical conversion funnel for a picks Discord? How long does someone consume free content before converting, and what's the trigger?
2. What free content hook is most effective at driving paid conversion — a big win, a consistent track record, a specific pick that hits, or something else?
3. What is the ideal onboarding experience for a new paid subscriber? What should they see and receive in the first 48 hours to reduce early churn?
4. What is the average lifetime value (LTV) of a paying picks subscriber, and what actions in the first 30 days predict whether they'll stay 3+ months?
5. How do re-engagement strategies work for cancelled subscribers — is it worth running win-back campaigns, and what makes them effective?
6. What does the research say about annual vs. monthly billing for picks services — does annual commitment increase LTV and reduce churn?

---

## Section 9 — What makes picksbyjonny specifically differentiated

**Questions:**
1. In the current picks service landscape, how many services use a genuine quantitative projection model (vs. gut feel, consensus, or copy-pasted lines)? What is the perceived value of "the model says" vs. "I think"?
2. What do bettors with a sharp/analytical orientation (the primary picksbyjonny audience) specifically want that mainstream tout services don't provide?
3. Is CLV (Closing Line Value) a concept that resonates with the target audience, or is it too technical? How do top sharp-oriented services communicate edge without losing people in the math?
4. Multi-sport coverage (NBA + MLB + WNBA + NHL) — is this a differentiator or a distraction for subscribers? Do they want a specialist or a generalist?
5. What is the value of Discord as a platform vs. alternatives (Substack, X, Telegram, proprietary app) for a sharp picks service in 2025-26?
6. Given the "edge > everything" positioning, what are the 3-5 things picksbyjonny must do exceptionally well to justify a paid subscription over free alternatives?

---

## Deliverable format

For each section, provide:
- **Key findings** (3-5 bullets of the most important, specific, actionable insights)
- **What this means for picksbyjonny** (1-2 sentences translating the finding to a specific recommendation)
- **Sources/basis** (what research, surveys, community discussions, or examples this is grounded in)

Prioritize findings that are surprising or counterintuitive — things that challenge default assumptions about what makes a picks service valuable.
