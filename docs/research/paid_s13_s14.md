## Section 13 — Book Affiliate and Additional Revenue Streams

### Key Findings

**1. Affiliate Commission Structures Are Meaningfully Different by Book and Deal Type**

- **DraftKings**: CPA $100–$300 (sportsbook), $200–$600 (casino), $40–$100 (DFS). Revenue share 25–40% of net gaming revenue (NGR). Offers hybrid (CPA + rev share).
- **FanDuel**: CPA $150–$300 per qualified depositor. Revenue share ~25–35% of net revenue.
- **BetMGM**: CPA up to $50/qualified player (notably lower than DraftKings/FanDuel), hybrid deals available.
- Industry-wide range: CPA $50–$600, rev share 20–50% of NGR. The spread is wide — a small picks service sending high-intent depositors gets the same or better rates than a traffic aggregator sending casual clickers.
- CONFIRMED: These are publicly documented commission ranges from affiliate program pages and affiliate network listings (sportsbookapi.com, 15m.com, statsdrone.com).

**2. Licensing Requirements Are Real and State-Specific — Do Not Ignore**

- DFS promotion (DraftKings DFS, FanDuel DFS) has **no licensing requirement** in most states. This is the cleanest affiliate channel.
- Sportsbook promotion in many US states requires an affiliate/vendor license. Examples:
  - New York: affiliate license required to promote sportsbooks (marketing affiliates do not need a full license but must contract with a licensed operator directly).
  - New Jersey: free vendor registration for CPA deals; $2,000 Ancillary CSIE license required for rev-share deals.
  - Many other states: similar framework — CPA is easier, rev-share triggers licensing.
- Colorado (where picksbyjonny operates): state gaming commission governs affiliate licensing. Colorado has affiliate licensing requirements for sportsbook promotion.
- INFERENCE: picksbyjonny should start with CPA deals or DFS affiliate (no license needed) while checking CO-specific requirements before running rev-share deals. Consult the Colorado Limited Gaming Control Commission before going live with sportsbook rev-share links.
- CONFIRMED source: bettingusa.com/affiliate, lineups.com legal US affiliate guide, ifrahlaw.com NY affiliate requirements.

**3. Affiliate Is a Secondary Income Stream for Picks Services, Not a Primary One**

- For picks services, subscription revenue is primary. Affiliate income is real but sporadic and secondary.
- Referral income is event-driven: a new depositor is worth $100–$300 CPA one time, or a percentage of their losses ongoing with rev-share. A subscriber who deposits $500 and bets for 6 months generates meaningful rev-share; a subscriber who deposits and quits after a week generates almost nothing on rev-share.
- RotoGrinders (DFS/picks hybrid) historically made significant revenue from DFS site referral fees — then pivoted to premium subscriptions as the industry matured. This is the standard trajectory: affiliate revenue is strong early in a market, then premium content becomes the core.
- INFERENCE: For a 100–500 member service, affiliate income at $100–$200 CPA per new depositor referral might generate $5,000–$30,000/year as a side stream, depending on how aggressively it's promoted. This is meaningful but not the business model.

**4. Natural Affiliate Integration: Best Practice Is "Recommended Book" Not "Sponsored Post"**

- The services that maintain credibility while running affiliates do it by:
  - Listing a "recommended books" or "where I bet" section with context (line shopping, promotions, interface quality) rather than just a signup link.
  - Disclosing the affiliate relationship in one clear sentence ("I earn a commission if you sign up through my link — doesn't affect my picks") placed prominently.
  - Mentioning specific books in the context of picks (e.g., "best number on this is DraftKings right now") — organic and credible.
  - Never making affiliate promotion the dominant content of a post or Discord channel.
- What kills credibility: rotating "link of the week" promotions, hard-selling books in the same message as picks, changing "recommended book" based on whoever is paying more that month.
- INFERENCE (based on community sentiment patterns): Discord communities and Reddit are highly attuned to when a service is shill-posting for books. The sharp/analytical brand makes this risk higher — a sell-out perception is harder to recover from than for a casual tout.

**5. FTC Disclosure Is Mandatory — Non-Compliance Risk Is Real**

- The FTC requires clear, prominent disclosure of any material connection (including affiliate commissions) for every piece of content that promotes a sportsbook.
- Placement requirements: above the fold on web content, first sentence of Discord posts with affiliate links, visible before the subscriber clicks.
- Language must be plain: "I earn a commission if you sign up." Not in fine print, not buried in a bio.
- Penalty exposure: as of 2025, up to $53,000 per violation. Each post without disclosure is a separate violation.
- CONFIRMED: FTC Endorsement Guides (consumer.ftc.gov), postaffiliatepro.com betting affiliate disclosure guide.

**6. Additional Revenue Streams: What Works vs. What Doesn't at Small Scale**

- **DFS content/lineups alongside picks**: RotoGrinders and Awesemo demonstrate this is viable. DFS tools subscribers pay $80–$150/month (DFS Hero pricing: $79.95 essential, $149.95 power). Adding DFS lineup output to a picks service can increase LTV if the audience overlaps. The NBA/WNBA calendar aligns well with picksbyjonny's sports coverage.
- **Bankroll management coaching/courses**: Market exists (YouTube education space, Udemy), but commoditized. Low differentiation unless the content is proprietary (e.g., teaching VAKE explicitly). One-off course pricing typically $97–$297 for betting education products.
- **1-on-1 consultation / VIP access**: Whop data shows services charging $500–$1,000/month for VIP tiers that include 1-on-1 access to the capper. Parlay Raj VIP at $1,000/month is a confirmed example. This is a high-LTV tier but requires significant time commitment from the operator.
- **Model licensing**: Selling access to projection data or model outputs to other cappers or DFS players. Not a common standalone product for small services; more relevant at 1,000+ member scale.
- **Merchandise**: Low priority early — margin is low and brand is too new. Relevant at brand maturity (2+ years, 1,000+ community members).
- CONFIRMED pricing data: Whop listings, DFS Hero pricing page, sacra.com Whop revenue research.

### What This Means for picksbyjonny

1. **Start with DFS affiliate links (no licensing) and CPA deals** — lowest legal friction. Before running any rev-share sportsbook affiliate links in Colorado, verify CO licensing requirements with the Limited Gaming Control Commission or a gaming attorney.

2. **Frame affiliate as "where I bet" not "sign up here"** — one dedicated #affiliates channel in Discord, natural mentions in picks posts when a book has a standout line. The sharp brand makes authenticity mandatory.

3. **Always disclose** — one sentence, every post with a link: "Affiliate link — I earn a commission at no cost to you." Build this into templates from day one.

4. **Don't build the business on affiliate income at 100–250 member scale** — it's $5,000–$20,000/year at best, not the core. Treat it as a bonus, not a revenue pillar.

5. **DFS lineups are the highest-potential upsell** — picksbyjonny's projection engine already produces player projections for NBA/WNBA. A DFS lineup output or optimizer-ready export could command an additional $20–$50/month from a subset of subscribers with very low incremental cost.

6. **A VIP/consultation tier at $200–$500/month for 5–10 members is the highest-ROI premium tier** — cap it to protect your time, promote it as exclusive access, and use it as a trial ground for what subscribers actually want to discuss.

## Section 14 — Subscriber Psychology and Expectation Management

### Key Findings

**1. Subscribers Do Not Have a Uniform Psychological Model — Three Distinct Archetypes Exist**

Research and community patterns across r/sportsbook, picks service review sites, and sports betting psychology literature identify three subscriber archetypes with very different churn risk:

- **The Bettor (high churn risk)**: Subscribed to win money. Treats picks as guaranteed outcomes. When they lose, they feel personally victimized — the service "cost me money." Blame is externalized immediately. This archetype cancels after 2–5 consecutive losing days.
- **The Student (moderate churn)**: Subscribed to learn process and improve. Tolerates variance better because they're evaluating the model, not just results. Engages with reasoning and bad-beat explanations. Churn trigger is loss of confidence in the operator's honesty, not a losing streak per se.
- **The DFS/Sharp Player (low churn)**: Has betting experience, understands variance, subscribes for edge on top of their own process. The most sophisticated and lowest-churn segment. They'll stay through a 2-week cold streak if the methodology is sound.
- INFERENCE: picksbyjonny's sharp/analytical branding is a natural filter — it attracts more Students and Sharps and repels pure Bettors. This is intentional and correct. Do not dilute the brand to attract Bettors.

**2. The "Losing Streak Cancellation Threshold" Is Approximately 3–5 Consecutive Losing Days for Casual Subscribers**

- No academic study with a precise number was found. Based on subscription SaaS churn research and sports betting psychology literature, the pattern is:
  - Day 1 loss: frustration, no action
  - Day 2–3 loss: doubt, may reduce stakes
  - Day 4–5 loss: emotional tilt, cancellation impulse
  - Day 6+ loss: very high cancellation probability for casual subscribers
- Professional/sophisticated subscribers can sustain 10–20 losing-day streaks if the service communicates clearly.
- Price point matters: subscribers at $100+/month have higher sunk-cost motivation to stay ("I'm not wasting this subscription") — but they also have higher expectations and cancel harder when those expectations aren't met.
- INFERENCE: This is why the first 30 days after sign-up are the highest-risk churn window. Onboarding that sets expectations before any picks are posted is critical.

**3. Framing Losses as Process Outcomes (Not Failures) Is the Single Highest-Impact Retention Lever**

- The most-cited framework in sports betting psychology content: separate the quality of a bet from the outcome. A "right call, wrong result" frame (the model identified a genuine edge that variance didn't convert) is substantively different from silence or excuse-making.
- Services that explain WHY a pick lost (box score context, bad beat event, line move against them post-post) retain subscribers significantly better than services that post results and move on.
- Key distinction: explaining a bad beat is credibility-building. Explaining every loss as a bad beat is excuse-making. Subscribers can tell the difference.
- The services with strongest retention language:
  - Acknowledge the loss directly and immediately
  - Provide one sentence of context (not a defense)
  - Redirect to the long-run record and edge thesis
  - Never project anger, frustration, or resentment at subscribers who complain
- CONFIRMED pattern: CapperTek reviews consistently cite "ghosting during losing streaks" as the #1 churn driver and "quick, honest communication" as the #1 retention factor.

**4. Unit-Based Sizing Significantly Reduces Emotional Churn vs. Flat Picks**

- When picks are framed as "2u on X" instead of "bet X," subscribers think in portfolio terms, not individual outcomes. A 2u loss feels different from a "100% loss."
- Unit-based sizing forces subscribers to think about their own bankroll management, which shifts psychological ownership from the service to the subscriber for stake sizing.
- Services using units with explicit sizing rationale ("2u because of high win prob, tight line") have materially lower post-loss complaint rates than flat-pick services.
- The Kelly Criterion / unit sizing education is a protective asset: subscribers who understand why their unit represents 1–2% of bankroll do not catastrophize a 5-unit losing week as a 5x disaster.
- CONFIRMED via betting education community consensus: docsports.com, optimal-bet.com, banking psychology literature on loss framing.

**5. The "I Lost Money on Your Pick" Complaint Is About Trust Violation, Not Money**

- Research on consumer psychology and loss aversion shows that people experience losses approximately 2x more intensely than equivalent gains (Kahneman/Tversky prospect theory). In a picks context, this manifests as: a losing day feels twice as bad as a winning day feels good.
- The "I lost money" complaint is almost never purely financial at the subscription price point ($30–$150/month). The actual emotional driver is one of:
  - Feeling misled about what the service delivers
  - Feeling that the operator doesn't care about their loss
  - Feeling embarrassed about following advice that lost
  - Feeling they could have done better without the service
- The correct response is not to defend the model — it is to acknowledge the outcome, express understanding of the frustration, and reiterate what the service actually provides (edge identification and process, not guaranteed wins).
- INFERENCE: A written "what to expect" document at onboarding — ideally requiring acknowledgment — reduces the "I was misled" complaint dramatically because the subscriber has explicitly agreed to the terms of the relationship.

**6. Bankroll Size Correlates Inversely With Churn During Losing Streaks**

- Subscribers with larger bankrolls (50–200+ units) experience individual losing streaks as a normal variance event. Subscribers with small bankrolls (5–15 units) experience the same number of losing picks as catastrophic drawdown.
- This is structural: a 5-unit losing week on a 100-unit bankroll is a 5% drawdown. On a 15-unit bankroll, it is a 33% drawdown and psychologically devastating.
- INFERENCE: This is the best argument for a subscriber qualification filter — not gatekeeping based on wealth, but communicating clearly that the service is designed for operators with 50+ unit bankrolls, and that undercapitalized subscribers will have a bad experience regardless of pick quality. Framing: "This service works best if you're playing with a dedicated betting bankroll of $500–$2,000+."
- INFERENCE: This also argues against marketing to people who are looking to "turn $50 into $500" — they will churn and leave negative reviews.

**7. The Correct Mental Model to Sell: "Best Available Information," Not "Winning Picks"**

- The fundamental trust contract that survives losing streaks: subscribers understand they are paying for analytical process and edge identification — the best available information given the model — and that variance determines outcomes from there.
- Services that sell "winning picks" or imply high win rates are creating a fragile trust contract that shatters on variance.
- Services that sell "edge and process" with transparent long-run records create a durable trust contract where a losing week is contextually interpreted as variance, not evidence of fraud.
- The framing distinction at the point of sale: "We find bets where the probability exceeds the implied odds. That edge compounds over hundreds of picks — not every week." This primes subscribers for variance tolerance before they experience it.
- CONFIRMED framing analysis: laythehouserm.com capper trust article, bravosixpick.com honest guide, oddsshopper.com variance explainer.

**8. Transparent Track Records Are Both the Best Retention Tool and the Best Acquisition Tool**

- COUNTERINTUITIVE FINDING: Showing losses visibly and prominently actually increases subscriber trust and acquisition, not decreases it. Subscribers have been burned by services that cherry-pick records. Verified losing tickets are rare enough to be a differentiator.
- CapperTek and community review sites consistently rate transparency of results tracking as the #1 quality signal for legitimate services.
- Recommended practice: post every graded result, including losing tickets, with a brief note. Monthly P&L summaries posted publicly (not just inside the community) serve as both retention communication and organic acquisition content.
- INFERENCE: picksbyjonny's existing pick_log.csv infrastructure and grade_picks.py system is already set up to generate this data. The monthly recap graphic is the right format — ensuring it's shared outside the community (Twitter/X, Discord public channel) maximizes its dual function.

---

### What This Means for picksbyjonny

1. **Build the "what you're paying for" expectation contract into onboarding** — a single-page document (or Discord message requiring a reaction to confirm reading) that defines: what the model does, what the win rate target is (~55–58%), what a losing week means in context, and what unit sizing represents. This one step eliminates the majority of "I lost money" complaints.

2. **Never go silent during losing streaks** — the worst thing a service can do is stop posting or become defensive. Post the picks, post the results, post a brief context note if it was a bad-beat loss. Even a 3-sentence "here's what happened" post dramatically reduces churn.

3. **Unit sizing with explicit rationale is the right default** — picksbyjonny already does this (1u–4u KILLSHOT). Reinforce the unit framework in onboarding so subscribers understand why their stake ≠ their subscription fee's value.

4. **Qualify your ideal subscriber explicitly** — the sharp brand does this implicitly, but stating "this service is designed for bettors who are already comfortable with unit-based bankroll management and understand variance" in marketing filters out the highest-churn demographic before they join.

5. **Invest in visible track record transparency** — monthly P&L graphics posted publicly (not just in the community) are the single highest-credibility signal. The existing grade_picks.py + results_graphic.py pipeline supports this today.

6. **Build a two-tier emotional response protocol**:
   - Losing day: post results with brief note, no defense required
   - Extended losing streak (5+ days): proactive message to community reiterating the long-run process, sharing the full-season record, and normalizing variance — before subscribers ask
