### 1. NBA per-game stat dispersion (sigma / NB-r) for PTS, AST, REB, 3PM — 2024-25 & 2025-26 regime

- **Audit claim**: PTS: sigma_mult=0.35 x mean with sigma_min=5.0 (a 25-PPG scorer gets sigma~8.75). AST: NB dispersion r; JonnyParlay r=12.16 vs projector r=9.65 (lower r = more variance). REB: JP r=14.7 vs projector r=13.16. 3PM: r=9.15. Var = mean + mean^2/r, so smaller r => fatter tails.
- **External finding**: No authoritative public table of per-stat game-to-game SD for star scorers exists (consistency articles paywall raw numbers, use composite Game Score / DFS points, or are stale), so dispersion was computed directly from a current-regime log. Using Luka Doncic's 2025-26 ESPN game log (n=64-65, mean ~33.5 PPG), the game-to-game PTS sample SD is ~9.2 (CV=0.275); the audit's 0.35 x mean would give ~11.7, roughly 25-35% wider than observed. The multiplicative form and floor are reasonable, but 0.35 sits above the elite-scorer empirical CV — which is expected, since lower-volume role players carry higher CV and a single mixed-population multiplier should sit above the star CV. For 3PM, Doncic's VMR was ~0.97-1.03 (near-Poisson at the single-player level); the audit's pooled var/mu=1.149 (r=9.15) is plausible for a population fit that mixes shooters and adds between-player overdispersion (Squared Statistics NB 3P article; Gonzalez). For AST (JP r=12.16 / var/mu=1.32 vs projector r=9.65), Doncic's VMR was ~1.13-1.20 — below the pooled 1.32 as expected, and single-player evidence cannot adjudicate between the two r values because the comparable quantity is the POPULATION var/mu, not a one-player r. For REB (JP r=14.7 / var/mu=1.39 vs 13.16), Doncic's VMR ~1.21-1.33 lines up with the audit band but cannot separate the two candidate r values. The verifier flagged that SD was computed on n=64-65 vs an 82-game season (so it understates full-distribution exposure), that basketballpoetry uses composite Game Score CV (a rank-ordering tool, explicitly "doesn't mean anything tangible in and of itself"), that the Gonzalez PDF was not machine-readable, and that Binomial Basketball / Columbia give no disclosed magnitudes — leaving three of four stats with ZERO external magnitude check.
- **Verdict**: PARTIALLY VERIFIED (unchanged from preliminary; confidence medium). PTS has a weak directional check consistent with literature; 3PM near-Poisson direction is corroborated; AST/REB are externally inconclusive.
- **Regime / caveats**: All anchors are deliberately 2025-26 to avoid stale pre-2020 pace/3PA data; 2025-26 is itself scoring/pace-elevated (Neil Paine), so 3PM means/variances may run slightly high. Single-player within-player VMR is structurally lower than a pooled population r and is NOT directly comparable to JonnyParlay's pooled NB_R; the comparable quantity is var/mean. Single-player n~64 carries ~±18% CI on SD, so exact r figures are illustrative only.
- **Implication for fix plan**: No threshold change. Do NOT lower PTS 0.35 toward 0.30-0.32 on a star-only sample — only a population-level CV fit by minutes/usage band (calibrate_distributions.py) is decisive, and the 5.0 floor governs the low-mean tail. Keep AST/REB/3PM on the internal game-level refit; external data cannot separate JP from projector r. Regime handling is correct.

### 2. MLB starting-pitcher OUTS dispersion (sigma multiplier), starts-only, 2024-25

- **Audit claim**: Current sigma_outs = 0.27 (a multiplier on projected outs; down from a historical 0.311 that was contaminated by relief appearances). Gate MIN_LEG_WIN_PROB_OUTS=0.62 was tuned to the OLD sigma=0.311 and is now too lax given the tighter 0.27. "Starts-only" is claimed to be the correct filter (vs including relief appearances).
- **External finding**: No source publishes SD-of-outs-per-start directly, so 0.27 was validated by reconstruction. MLB starters averaged ~5.24 IP/start early 2024 and ~5.3 IP/start full-season (FanGraphs "State of Starters in 2024"; StatMuse), i.e. ~15.7-15.9 outs/start, on a declining trend (~5.7 IP in 2008-2019 → ~5.0 in 2020-2023 → 5.24-5.3 in 2024). Shape: only 39.8% of starts reached >=6 IP (down from 60.7% in 2013), and QS% fell from 54.5% (2015) to 43.9% (2024) (CBS/Royals Review; WKU IJES; Crawford Street Journal). With mean ~15.8 outs and the >=18-out threshold near the ~60th percentile of a left-skewed, capped distribution, the implied start-to-start SD lands roughly 1.3-1.8 IP (~4.0-5.4 outs). At ~16 projected outs, 0.27 implies SD ~4.3 outs (~1.43 IP) — inside that band; the old 0.311 implies ~5.0 outs (~1.66 IP), consistent with upward contamination from short relief outings. The sabermetric standard (IP/GS) by construction excludes relief; FanGraphs classifies a pitcher as a starter only when starts exceed relief appearances — confirming starts-only is correct. The verifier added that the engine's OWN DB confirms the mechanism more strongly than the external reconstruction: calibrated.py documents within-player CV starts-only=0.228 vs relief=0.443 on 16,187 starts / 345 pitchers. Three weaknesses keep this short of VERIFIED: (1) the finding validated 0.27 against POOLED dispersion (CV ~0.276), but the per-pitcher SIGMA multiplier should track the WITHIN-player CV (0.228) — 0.27 is intentionally set ABOVE within-CV as a left-tail buffer, not "squarely inside" a per-pitcher band; (2) the 39.8% >=6IP anchor is a 2023 figure mis-attributed to 2024; (3) no primary SD-of-outs-per-start exists.
- **Verdict**: PARTIALLY VERIFIED (unchanged; confidence medium). Direction (0.311→0.27) and starts-only filter supported; precise 0.27 magnitude is pooled-anchored, not externally validated against a per-pitcher band.
- **Regime / caveats**: IP/start is in active recent regime change; pre-2020 figures (6+ IP norm) are not authoritative — only 2024-2025 anchors used. The mean has crept slightly UP from the 2020-2023 trough, modestly raising absolute SD in outs at fixed multiplier. Normal-vs-skew concern is real near the 18-out QS line (over-allocates >7IP tail, under-fits early-KO lower tail); the engine's empirical-CDF/July-refit candidate is the right resolution.
- **Implication for fix plan**: Keep starts-only (confirmed by in-DB within-CV 0.228 vs relief 0.443). The 0.311→0.27 tightening is directionally correct. Treat 0.27 as a pooled-CV-anchored choice with a deliberate left-tail buffer — NOT externally validated for the per-pitcher object. MIN_LEG_WIN_PROB_OUTS=0.62 being too lax under the tighter sigma is internally consistent, but external data cannot set the gate; resolve via the existing n>=40 graded-OUTS-SGP-leg monitor (sigma-equivalent floor ~0.64). Retune the gate upward only when that monitor fires — do not hand-set it now.

### 4. Platt (sigmoid) scaling vs isotonic / beta / temperature calibration; defensible minimum sample size

- **Audit claim**: Audit found the Combo (SGP) and MLB Platt calibrators are MISSING. Implicit assumption: Platt scaling is the right calibration method. Audit did not specify a minimum sample size for a reliable Platt fit.
- **External finding**: Platt (sigmoid) scaling is well-supported as the right method for small calibration samples — exactly the JonnyParlay regime (Combo 27/100, MLB 28/100, native H3 98/100). Niculescu-Mizil & Caruana (ICML 2005) found that with 1000+ calibration points isotonic always matches or beats Platt; below that, the 2-param Platt fit is preferred. scikit-learn codifies this: isotonic "will perform as well as or better than 'sigmoid' when there is enough data (greater than ~1000 samples)," sigmoid "is most effective for small sample sizes," and isotonic "is more prone to overfitting, especially on small datasets." On the 1-param vs 2-param distinction, Guo et al. (2017) show temperature scaling — "a single-parameter variant of Platt Scaling" — is "surprisingly effective" and often beats the multi-parameter version when data is scarce, consistent with the H3 plan's intercept-only fit at n=76-100 and free 2-param fit at n>=300. Beta calibration (Kull et al. 2017) is a 3-param generalization that corrects distortions Platt cannot, but is MORE data-hungry and does not displace Platt at small n. The verifier confirmed the scikit-learn quotes verbatim and the characterizations of Guo and Kull, but bounded the verdict: (1) all primary sources are UCI/benchmark/neural-net classification, not sports-betting probabilities — the domain transfer is reasonable but an extrapolation, and the cited sports sources (OpticOdds blog, iWinRNFL) are weak/tangential; (2) no source pins an absolute minimum n for a stable 2-param Platt fit — the n>=300 cutover is JonnyParlay's own, supported only by ANALOGY to Guo. The finding states both caveats honestly and does not over-claim.
- **Verdict**: VERIFIED (unchanged; confidence high) for the method-choice claim. The specific sample-size thresholds are supported-by-analogy, not literature-pinned.
- **Regime / caveats**: None statistical. Note the evidence base is general ML classifier calibration, not betting markets specifically; treat the domain transfer as an informed assumption.
- **Implication for fix plan**: No reprioritization. Do NOT switch to isotonic or beta at current n (all ~10-40x below the ~1000 crossover). Keep the 100-row gate as a floor; use 1-param intercept-only fits for Combo/MLB until n>=300 (mirroring native H3); revisit isotonic only if a per-market set exceeds ~1000 graded rows. Defer final threshold choices to the user.

### 5. SGP correlation (rho) estimation: n=100 threshold stability and pitcher-OUTS vs opposing-HITS correlation

- **Audit claim**: MLB SGP rho uses structural priors, awaiting 100+ observed SGPs before empirical refit. rho=0.30 for OUTS x opposing-HITS is called the "most consequential" value. Validate that n=100 is a defensible threshold for rho estimation stability, and what literature says about pitcher-outs vs opposing-hits correlation.
- **External finding**: (1) Sample size: the Fisher z standard error is SE_z = 1/sqrt(n-3), independent of r (Wikipedia; SAS). At n=100, SE_z ≈ 0.1015, giving a 95% z-CI of ±~0.199; for rho=0.30 the back-transformed rho CI is roughly [0.11, 0.47] — reliably positive but loose. The canonical stability reference, Schönbrodt & Perugini (2013, J. Research in Personality 47:609-612), reports a point of stability of ~n=161 for a ±0.10 corridor (80% confidence) and that stricter confidence "should approach 250." So n=100 is defensible only as a coarse sign-and-rough-magnitude check, below both thresholds. (2) Direction/magnitude: there is no peer-reviewed published coefficient for SP outs vs same-game opposing hits, but the structural mechanism is unambiguous — more hits raises WHIP, produces more baserunners, and pulls a starter earlier, so raw OUTS-vs-HITS is NEGATIVE; the audit's +0.30 is positive because it pairs OUTS-OVER with opposing-HITS-UNDER (both betting the same "pitcher dominance" latent factor), exactly consistent with the negative raw relationship. SGP-pricing sources confirm material leg correlation and 20-40% cross-book pricing variance (OpticOdds); Wizard of Odds' illustrative matrix runs ~0.28-0.42 for related legs (NFL-illustrative, not MLB-empirical). The verifier confirmed the Fisher arithmetic, the Schönbrodt & Perugini paper and ~250 figure, and that mlb_sgp_builder.py:204-212 returns rho=0.30 for OUTS-over × opposing-HITS-under. Key weakness the finding underplays: logged SGPs are gate-selected and same-game-correlated, so effective n is BELOW nominal 100 — making n=100 even weaker than the clean-IID CI implies.
- **Verdict**: PARTIALLY VERIFIED (unchanged; confidence high). Sign is structurally well-founded; n=100 supports sign/coarse-magnitude only; the 0.30 magnitude is empirically unvalidated.
- **Regime / caveats**: None season-specific. The Fisher/Schönbrodt machinery assumes IID bivariate-normal observations, which gate-selected same-game SGPs violate — effective n < 100.
- **Implication for fix plan**: Keep "wait for empirical data," but treat the n=100 refit explicitly as a sign/coarse-magnitude check, not a precise magnitude (which needs ~160-250 observed SGPs). Keep rho=0.30 as a shrinkage anchor (empirical-Bayes blend of observed r toward the 0.30 prior until n≈160+). Flag the OUTS×HITS magnitude as the single value most worth tightening. No code change to the sign.

### 6. Negative-binomial dispersion parameter convention (r vs alpha=1/r)

- **Audit claim**: Audit assumes NB_R is the dispersion r with Var = mean + mean^2/r. JP and projector use different r for AST and REB. Confirm the math convention is internally consistent and that no path conflates r with alpha=1/r.
- **External finding**: Both standard parameterizations are confirmed by primary/official sources. The "size"/shape convention (var = mu + mu^2/r, r the gamma shape — R's MASS::glm.nb "Theta", brms "shape", scipy.stats.nbinom's "n") gives n = r = mu^2/(var - mu), the exact inverse of var = mu + mu^2/r. Hilbe's NB2 uses Var = mu + mu^2/theta ("small theta means heavy overdispersion; as theta grows the model slides back toward Poisson"). The regression "alpha" convention (statsmodels NB2: Var = mu + alpha*mu^2) is the reciprocal, alpha = 1/r = 1/theta (Soenning). Codebase check: engine/quant/distributions.py negbinom_pmf(k, mu, r) implements p = r/(r+mu), n = r — the scipy/R "size" convention; calibrate_distributions.py _nb_r(mu, var) = mu^2/(var - mu) (inf if var<=mu) is the exact inverse; prob_core.py routes NB_STATS to negbinom_cdf with r = NB_R[stat]/NB_R_WNBA passed directly. CLAUDE.md always reports larger r at lower var/mu (3PM r=9.15 at var/mu=1.1486 vs WNBA 3PM r=1.342 at var/mu=1.708), consistent with r=size not alpha. No path divides by NB_R to form an alpha or passes NB_R into a var=mu+alpha*mu^2 form. AST/REB use independent r (NBA AST 12.16, REB 14.7; WNBA AST 11.37, REB 10.74) that never cross-interact. ### 8. Monotonicity principle: lower model/tier trust should require a larger edge cushion / hurdle

- **Audit claim**: Internal rule: tiers with lower Baker-McHale shrinkage trust must have higher edge floors (less-trusted => more cushion). Audit found a violation: T3 has the lowest BM weight (0.70) but only a mid-pack edge floor (0.06), while more-trusted tiers have higher floors. Validate the underlying principle against external literature.
- **External finding**: The directional principle — when an estimate is less trustworthy, demand a larger margin of safety and/or stake less — is well-established and rooted in the same Baker & McHale work the engine names its shrinkage after. (1) Baker & McHale (2013, INFORMS Decision Analysis): Kelly ignores win-probability uncertainty; "the size of the bet should be shrunk in the presence of this parameter uncertainty," and the optimal full-Kelly fraction decreases as uncertainty rises — the direct analogue of the engine's BM weight. (2) Bailey & Lopez de Prado, "The Deflated Sharpe Ratio" (2014): the decision threshold is RAISED to account for estimation error, selection bias, sample length — the finance analogue of "less reliable signal => higher hurdle." (3) Bayesian model averaging: averaging is itself a shrinkage estimator; naive multimodel intervals are "overconfident, providing overly narrow intervals" — uncertainty should WIDEN the safety band. (4) Walsh & Joshi (2024, arXiv:2303.06021): emphasizes calibration over accuracy and recommends raising the EV threshold and abstaining under high uncertainty ("as the Expected Value Threshold increases so does the return on investment"). Magnitude caveat: a Downey simulation finds the shrinkage is often modest at moderate uncertainty — moving win-prob sigma from 5% to 20% only cut the optimal Kelly fraction from 0.40 to 0.36 for a 70/30 bet; only extreme uncertainty (sigma~50%) materially changes bet size — so direction is principled but large step changes for small trust differences are not endorsed. The verifier confirmed all sources verbatim (Baker & McHale thesis, Walsh & Joshi +34.69% vs -35.17% ROI and the EV-threshold/ROI relationship, the Downey figures, the Deflated Sharpe Ratio) and DOWNGRADED the verdict on over-claiming grounds: (1) the literature validates the general direction but NOT the specific cross-tier rule that BM-weight ordering must map onto edge-floor ordering — stake and hurdle are substitutable safety levers, so a mid-pack T3 floor (0.06) is not per se a violation if T3's stake multiplier already shrinks more; (2) the engine's own Plan-10 "BM direction inverted" note (T1 highest weight 0.75 but worst empirical ROI) means the trust ranking the rule keys on is itself contested, making the flagged "violation" indeterminate; (3) Downey shows the effect is weak at moderate uncertainty, so the existing 0.05/0.06/0.07 spacing may already be adequate.

The directional principle is solidly supported; the audit's specific monotonicity-violation claim is INCONCLUSIVE pending the n>=150 per-family bootstrap refit. Missing evidence: no source endorses a cross-tier BM-weight↔edge-floor ordering constraint, and the realized-ROI trust ranking is empirically contested.
- **Regime / caveats**: None statistical.
